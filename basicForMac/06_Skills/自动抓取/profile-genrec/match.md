# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of each item for readers researching generative recommendation: large language models combined with recommender systems, generative retrieval and ranking, LLM-based user modeling, controllable content generation for recommendations, RAG for recommendation, and new datasets, benchmarks, or evaluation protocols in this area.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** New paradigm-level results, major model releases from leading labs or companies, landmark papers, or industry deployments that change how recommendations are generated.
- **7-8: High value.** Solid new methods, important benchmark or dataset releases, significant open-source projects, in-depth analyses, or major updates to widely used recommender frameworks with generative components.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Incremental improvements, applied case studies, tutorials, workshops, or moderate-interest results worth knowing.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine releases, shallow coverage, promotional content, or work only loosely related to recommendation.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, off-topic material, trivial updates, or purely promotional content.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider technical novelty, experimental rigor, relevance to generative recommendation, source quality, and concrete supporting details. Generic LLM news without a recommendation or retrieval angle is not relevant. Treat strong community discussion as added evidence, but do not equate popularity with importance. Do not reward exaggerated headlines.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
