### [Evaluating Brand Retrieval and Ranking in Large Language Model Recommendations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16304) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new arXiv paper proposes a framework for evaluating open-ended brand recommendations generated by large language models \(LLMs\). The authors define the competitive set independently of model outputs and estimate recommendation prevalence and prominence through repeated sampling, operationalizing these constructs with Brand Recommendation Probability \(BRP@k\) and Mean Reciprocal Rank \(MRR@k\). Applying the framework to six LLMs across five product categories, they find that category-only queries substantially omit established brands and that recommendation prominence shows limited evidence of following conventional brand popularity; instead, prominence is associated with broader marketplace-visibility signals such as search interest and online brand conversation. Needs-based queries change which brands are retrieved, and diagnostic positioning probes show that brands omitted from ordinary recommendations can remain conditionally retrievable when distinctive cues are supplied. The authors provide open-source software and data for reproducible evaluation.

rss · arXiv cs.IR · Sep 16, 04:00

**「Background」** LLMs are increasingly used for product recommendation, but evaluating their outputs differs from conventional information retrieval and recommender systems. LLMs can generate recommendations without an explicit candidate set, and repeated responses to the same query can produce different brands and rankings. The paper addresses these evaluation challenges by treating LLM recommendation as a stochastic retrieval-and-ranking process rather than judging individual generated lists.

**「Impact」** The framework introduces concrete metrics \(BRP@k and MRR@k\) for open-ended LLM brand recommendations and reports empirical results across six LLMs and five product categories. Its findings suggest that brand visibility in LLM recommendations may not track conventional brand popularity and can shift with query context, which has implications for how brands, researchers, and platforms assess and compare LLM-generated recommendations. The paper also releases open-source software and data to support reproducible evaluation.

### [Wolt&\#x27;s Universal Venue Ranker: A Hybrid Transformer-GBDT Ranker for On-Demand Delivery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16407) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Wolt presents the Universal Venue Ranker \(UVR\), a production store-ranking system that pairs a bidirectional transformer encoder for sequential user modeling with a GBDT ranker integrating contextual, user, and store features. Trained across all stores and domains of a country while enforcing local delivery constraints at inference, UVR replaces four previously separate ranking models \(three for restaurants, one for retail\) with a single unified system. To balance surfacing new stores against preserving reorder quality, the authors apply label smoothing and trial-biased sample weighting, reporting offline trial MRR improvements of +12% to +30% over production while regressing reorder MRR in five of six countries. These regressions leave Global CVR, the core online metric blending trial and reorder sessions, statistically unchanged. The system was validated in three consecutive A/B tests: UVR V1 delivers +5.5% Merchant Trial Rate and +0.16% Global CVR over the previous production ranker; V2 adds a further +0.45% Merchant Trial Rate; and V3, the cross-domain unification of restaurant and retail rankers, adds a further +1.31% Retail Merchant Trial Rate.

### [Pre-retrieval Query Clustering for Adaptive Top-k Document Retrieval in RAG Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13489) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This paper proposes a query-adaptive retrieval-depth framework for RAG systems that replaces fixed top-k retrieval. Offline, it estimates per-query retrieval difficulty by measuring NDCG under the default retriever and deriving a query-specific saturation point k\* from the NDCG-k curve. Because computing these signals online is expensive, it clusters a large set of queries in embedding space and summarizes each cluster with a recommended retrieval depth targeting roughly 95% coverage via a mean-plus-variance rule. At runtime, an incoming query is assigned to a cluster and selects the corresponding top-k in constant time. The authors report that the framework has been tested in full-traffic queries, improving F1 by over 36% while reducing token usage by 14% on low-complexity clusters without accuracy loss.

### [P3Rec: Distilling Prior–Posterior Preference Reasoning for LLM-based Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13993) ⭐️ 7.0/10

P3Rec is a proposed framework for LLM-based recommendation that jointly extracts and internalizes complementary prior and posterior preference reasoning knowledge. The paper argues that existing LLM-as-Enhancer methods distill LLM-derived preference knowledge into lightweight recommenders from only one perspective: prior preference captures stable, consistent user interests but gives limited guidance for the current decision, while posterior preference reveals target-relevant fine-grained interests but may rely excessively on target clues. P3Rec derives target-agnostic prior preferences and target-conditioned posterior preferences from the user side, and also extracts item-centric preference representations from item semantics and predecessor interactions. It then progressively internalizes prior and posterior knowledge into behavioral representations via prior preference absorption and posterior-guided preference distillation. Because the resulting comprehensive preference representation may not always provide an equally decisive retrieval direction, P3Rec characterizes historical interest dispersion with interest entropy and adaptively calibrates the user representation before contrastive retrieval optimization. The abstract states that extensive experiments on multiple public datasets demonstrate effectiveness, but the supplied content does not include specific results.

The work targets modern search and recommendation settings that must return a coherent set of complementary results—such as a tent, sleeping bag, portable stove, and headlamp for a &quot;camping gear&quot; query—rather than near-duplicate matches. **「Background」** Modern search and recommendation applications are increasingly expected to return a coherent set of results rather than a single best match. To achieve this, systems use query fan-out, which breaks a single broad prompt into several related sub-queries to cover potential user interests. However, teaching an LLM to perform database-aware query decomposition dynamically drains a massive thinking budget: zero-shot LLMs are general autoregressive text predictors not optimized to navigate the specific geometric manifold of a target corpus, so they need extended test-time computation to return results that optimize higher-order set-level properties \(diversity, coverage, complementarity, coherence\) while remaining grounded to a fixed database. The work is described in an ICML 2026 paper, &quot;Efficient, Property-Aligned Fan-Out Retrieval via RL-Compiled Diffusion,&quot; which addresses this decomposition bottleneck via a reward-to-data compilation framework.

**「Impact」** Retrieve-for-Train uses offline reinforcement learning to discover reward-aligned fan-outs and compile them into supervision, then distills these optimized exploration behaviors into a lightweight diffusion retriever for single-pass query fan-out at inference time. The pipeline fine-tunes 4B open-source language models \(Gemma3-4B and Qwen3-4B\) via RL using a strict mathematical composite reward balancing three competing pillars, optimized with group relative policy optimization \(GRPO\) with soft proximal policy optimization \(PPO\). The authors note that this triad acts as mutual counter-anchors: optimizing purely for groundedness can lead to degenerate strings that map to a database coordinate, while adding alignment alone can collapse into repetitive paraphrases; injecting the Vendi Score as a counter-anchor closes off these shortcuts. Evaluation used frozen, dataset-specific multimodal embedding backbones and open-source language models across two set-valued retrieval regimes: a large-scale fashion dataset of user-curated outfits for text-to-image experiments \(CLIP-based retriever\) and a proprietary industrial dataset of expert-generated music playlists for text-to-music evaluations \(MuLan\). The fan-out models generated exactly 10 sub-queries per main prompt. Across both retrieval tasks, Retrieve-for-Train reportedly outperformed traditional single-query search, zero-shot expansion, and a heavily optimized Best-of-N baseline. Qualitatively, zero-shot LLM baselines tended to generate near-synonymous paraphrases \(e.g., &quot;bohemian festival style&quot; vs. &quot;bohemian festival fashion&quot;\), causing redundant results, while Retrieve-for-Train generated highly diverse, distinct sub-queries \(e.g., branching into &quot;boots&quot; or &quot;lace&quot;\) that remained strictly grounded within the database manifold. Directly deploying the RL-tuned language model yielded high search quality but inherited autoregressive latency constraints and a high computational thinking budget. Distilling that behavior into the 53.9M-parameter Retrieve-for-Train diffusion model reportedly delivered a 12 to 20 speedup over autoregressive approaches: while autoregressive fan-out latency expands linearly to nearly 50 seconds under large context batches, Retrieve-for-Train-Diffusion stays between sub-second to a few seconds.
