### [Evaluating Brand Retrieval and Ranking in Large Language Model Recommendations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16304) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This paper introduces a framework for evaluating open-ended brand recommendations generated by large language models \(LLMs\). Because LLMs can produce recommendations without an explicit candidate set and can vary their outputs across repeated queries, the authors define the competitive set independently of model outputs and estimate recommendation prevalence and prominence through repeated sampling. They operationalize these constructs with Brand Recommendation Probability \(BRP@k\) and Mean Reciprocal Rank \(MRR@k\), applying the framework to six LLMs across five product categories. Category-only queries reveal substantial omission of established brands and limited evidence that recommendation prominence follows conventional brand popularity; instead, prominence appears associated with broader marketplace-visibility signals, particularly search interest and online brand conversation. Needs-based queries show that contextualizing users&\#x27; goals and constraints changes which brands are retrieved, and diagnostic positioning probes indicate that brands omitted from ordinary recommendations can remain conditionally retrievable when distinctive cues are supplied. The authors provide open-source software and data for reproducible evaluation.

The paper addresses this gap by proposing an evaluation framework that treats LLM recommendation as a stochastic retrieval-and-ranking process rather than judging individual generated lists. ### [ASC and K-ASC: Efficient Approximate Swing Computation for Large-Scale Recommender Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16850) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This arXiv paper by Runhao Jiang and Renchi Yang addresses the computational cost of Swing scores, a user-item-user interaction-based similarity measure used for item-to-item retrieval in industrial recommender systems. The authors note that existing Swing computation methods are either prohibitively expensive due to quadratic time complexity with respect to item degree, or rely on truncation heuristics that yield unsatisfactory quality, making them impractical on graphs with billions of interactions.

The paper introduces ASC and K-ASC, two algorithms for approximate and top-K Swing queries. According to the abstract, these algorithms provide rigorous theoretical guarantees in probabilistic relative and additive errors of Swing values. ASC combines two randomized algorithms, GNS and USS, in what the authors describe as a simple yet non-trivial way to adaptively process high- and low-degree query items with minimal runtime cost. K-ASC targets top-K queries through a filter-refinement paradigm with carefully designed heuristics.

The abstract reports extensive experiments over eight real datasets, where ASC and K-ASC achieve orders of magnitude speed-up over competitors in computational time while offering the same approximate and top-K query result quality. K-ASC is described as highly efficient on massive graphs, including the billion-edge Yambda and MAG datasets. The Swing score is one such measure: given a user-item graph G, a query item v\_q, and a target item v\_t, sw\(v\_q, v\_t\) uses the user-item-user interaction structure to evaluate item similarity. According to the paper, Swing has proven highly effective for i2i retrieval and is widely used in industrial-scale recommender systems.

### [Pre-retrieval Query Clustering for Adaptive Top-k Document Retrieval in RAG Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13489) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This paper proposes a query-adaptive retrieval-depth framework for RAG systems that replaces fixed top-k retrieval, which the authors argue over-retrieves for simple queries and under-retrieves for complex ones. Offline, the method estimates per-query retrieval difficulty by measuring NDCG under the default retriever and derives a query-specific saturation point k\* from the NDCG-k curve. Because computing these signals online is expensive, it clusters a large set of queries in embedding space and summarizes each cluster with a recommended retrieval depth targeting high coverage \(e.g., ~95%\) via a mean-plus-variance rule. At runtime, an incoming query is assigned to a cluster and its corresponding top-k is selected in constant time. The authors state the framework has been tested in full-traffic queries, reporting an F1 improvement of over 36% while reducing token usage by 14% on low-complexity clusters without accuracy loss. The supplied abstract is truncated and reports no datasets, baselines, or detailed empirical results.

rss · arXiv cs.IR · Sep 16, 04:00

**「Background」** RAG systems commonly retrieve a fixed number of documents \(top-k\) to ground generation. The paper frames this static approach as brittle: simple queries suffer over-retrieval, adding noise and cost, while complex queries are under-retrieved, causing recall failures that cascade into incorrect answers. The work is motivated by the question of how many documents must be retrieved to answer an arbitrary query reliably. It positions its approach as pre-retrieval and query-centric, in contrast to post-retrieval confidence methods that rely on clustering retrieved documents, and claims robustness in heterogeneous, case-like corpora with applicability across domains such as legal, healthcare, finance, and enterprise search.

**「Potential Impact」** The framework targets retrieval and recommender practitioners seeking to make retrieval depth adaptive rather than fixed. Its reported full-traffic test claims an F1 improvement of over 36% and a 14% reduction in token usage on low-complexity clusters without accuracy loss. Because the supplied abstract is truncated and provides no datasets, baselines, or detailed empirical results, these claims cannot be independently assessed from the available material.
